Witch Caper

A VR puzzle adventure where you shrink and grow with a magic potion to rescue your cat from a witch's tower.

Overview

The Wicked Cat Caper is a VR game made by a team of four. A witch has locked your cat in a golden cage at the top of her tower. To get it back, you travel through a magical world with one tool: a potion that lets you glow and change your size. Letters from a mysterious guide point you through each trial, from lifting boulders to slipping past a spinning windmill.

Size changes more than how big you are. When you grow, you get strong enough to lift heavy things but move slowly and can't jump. When you shrink, you can slip through tiny gaps and the world around you seems to slow down. Anything golden can be resized too, which turns objects into tools.

Goal of the Project

We wanted a VR world where changing size is the main way to solve puzzles. That meant every space had to work at two scales: small enough to explore hidden corners, and big enough to lift heavy objects. My job was to build that world so it looked magical and still guided players to where they needed to go.

Inspiration

Stray showed us how a low, cat-sized view can change the way a world feels. We also looked at puzzle exploration games where you learn by playing instead of reading instructions. The witch and the cats came from our own earlier class projects, and the cats became a handy way for players to judge their own size.

My Role

Environment

  • World building — built the scene in Unity, from the witch's house and tower to the paths between them, using online and Unity Asset Store packs alongside my own models.
  • Puzzle spaces — laid out the river, statue area and windmill so each trial reads clearly at both small and large sizes.
  • Props & guides — placed the potion, statues and guide cats that lead players forward.

Visual Design & Modeling

  • 3D models — modeled the cage, golden key, flying island and guide letter myself.
  • Visual style — kept the world soft, low-poly and storybook-like, with gold for anything important.
  • Guide letters — designed the old-paper letters that tell the story and give hints.

How to Play

Each trial is built around the potion. Drink it to shrink or grow, then change back before moving on.

  1. Clear the path

    Grow big enough to lift the boulders blocking your way.

  2. Cross the river

    Find something glowing and golden, make it bigger and lay it across the water as a bridge.

  3. Match the statues

    Follow the colored sign and move each heavy statue back to its spot.

  4. Pass the windmill

    Shrink down so you move faster and the world slows, then slip past the blades.

  5. Find the key

    Stay small inside the witch's house and follow the soft glow to the golden key, then unlock the cage.

Storyboard

Before building anything, we sketched how each area would teach one rule. The onboarding shows the player that big means strong and small means access, and that anything golden can be resized. Trial 1 turns that into a puzzle: grow big, move the heavy pieces to match the map, and a path to the tower appears.

Three Sizes

The same spot by the river, seen at each size. I placed trees, rocks and the floating windmill so the view stays readable from the grass and from above the treetops.

Environment

The world follows a soft, medieval fantasy look. Outside, open forest and big set pieces show off scale. Inside the tower, tight spaces and small details make you look closely. Here is the path in the order players walk it.

3D Models

I modeled the four key story objects. The cage and key share the same polished gold, so players connect them right away. The flying island is low-poly to match the rest of the world. The other assets came from online sources and the Unity Asset Store.

Guide Letters

Instead of on-screen text, hints come as rolled-up letters that players pick up and open in VR. They fit the fairytale mood and give each trial a little story.

The Twist

In the last letter, the witch admits she is really a nature fairy. Many cats in her forest had been abandoned, so she set up the trials to see if you would truly go to any length for yours. Because you never gave up, she returns your cat with its collar.

Playtesting

Friends and classmates played it first with no explanation, then again with a short intro if they got stuck.

What Players Said

  • The trials flowed naturally from one to the next.
  • The onboarding taught the size rules quickly.
  • The windmill stood out most, because shrinking really did make it feel slower.
  • Some players weren't sure which objects they could use.

What We Changed

  • Added visual and on-screen hints.
  • Made interactive objects stand out more clearly.
  • Improved sound feedback so actions felt responsive.
  • Smoothed the pacing between areas.

Challenges & Reflection

Challenges

  • Designing spaces that work when the player is tiny and when they are giant.
  • Guiding players in VR without a map, using light, gold and placement instead.
  • Keeping my own models in the same style as the asset packs around them.
  • Keeping a large scene running smoothly enough for VR.

Reflection

Witch Caper showed me that in VR, the environment does the teaching. Players look around before they read anything, so a glowing key or a golden bridge has to say "use me" on its own. Modeling the cage and key myself also taught me how small details, like the cat in the key handle, make a world feel made with care.

Next time, I'd add more trials, push the time mechanic further and spend more time on visual polish and small story details in the world.
